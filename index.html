How we train

Four steps from first practice to competition day.

1

Diagnose

A short readiness assessment shows where the student stands today, by topic and by skill, for the contest they have in mind.

2

Plan

We map a season plan backward from the contest date, with weekly goals the student and family can see.

3

Simulate

Timed mock exams, buzzer rounds and practice debates mirror the real format, then we review every miss together.

4

Compete

A final tune-up on pacing, nerves and logistics, so competition day feels familiar instead of new.

Questions families ask

My student has never competed. Is that okay?

Yes. Many students start with us before their first contest. The assessment sets a realistic starting point and the plan builds from there.

Can a student prepare for more than one contest?

Yes. Many students pair contests that share subjects or skills, such as National Science Bowl with UIL science. Because each competition has its own format, students preparing for multiple contests should sign up for practice built for each one. We plan the season so the workloads don't collide.

Do you offer online or in-person sessions?

Both. Students can train online or in person, whichever fits your family's schedule. We also partner with schools to run after-school sessions for school teams.

Is coaching individual or for teams?

Either. Individual coaching focuses on one student's gaps and goals, which suits AMC prep and solo events like Original Oratory. Team coaching builds roles, communication and strategy for events like Science Bowl and debate, and works for school teams, clubs and friend groups.
